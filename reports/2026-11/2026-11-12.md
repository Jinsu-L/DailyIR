### 1. When Direction Matters: Diagnosing Relational Instruction Following in Dense Retrieval

- **LLM Score**: 0
- **Keyword Score**: 14
- **Authors**: Zhanyu Wu, Richong Zhang
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15417v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-11-11 12:44:22
- **Topic Keywords**: retriever, dense retrieval, query, relevance, retrieval
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### T.A.R.G.E.T. Summary (from Full Text)
- **Topic**: Relational Instruction Following in Dense Retrieval
- **Aim**: Diagnose and improve dense retrievers’ ability to obey natural‑language instructions that hinge on the direction of a relation between two entities.
- **Rationale**: Dense retrievers handle explicit constraints but consistently fail when relevance depends solely on relation direction (e.g., "A causes B" vs. "B causes A"); existing benchmarks rarely isolate this relational role‑binding effect, hindering targeted improvements.
- **Ground**: SwitchIR benchmark: 700 controlled groups (1,400 docs, 2,700 instruction conditions) and 140 realistic groups (280 docs, 520 conditions). Each group contains a fixed query, two hard‑negative docs, and two instruction realizations (canonical vs. meaning‑preserving) across three contrast types—Constraint Shift, Explicit Filtering, Relational Flip. Metrics: PairAcc, FlipBoth, ParaAll, ContrastAll, measuring anchor and all‑realization correctness.
- **Experiment**: Zero‑shot evaluation of embedding retrievers (Qwen3‑Emb, E5‑Mistral, GritLM, Promptriever), late‑interaction (BGE‑M3‑ColBERT), lexical baseline (BM25), and cross‑encoder rerankers (Qwen3‑Rerank‑4B/8B). Results: BM25 achieves perfect anchor accuracy but fails all‑realization metrics; embedding models perform poorly on Relational Flip; cross‑encoder rerankers improve Relational Flip but still lag behind Explicit Filtering. Fine‑tuning on SwitchIR boosts direction‑sensitivity, especially in larger models.
- **Takeaway**: Dense retrievers excel on simple cue‑based preferences but struggle with direction‑sensitive relational instructions; direction‑sensitive signals are weak and often overridden by default pair preference. SwitchIR exposes this diagnostic gap, and fine‑tuning—particularly with larger models—enhances generalization to unseen relation families, guiding future research toward robust relational instruction following.

#### Abstract
> Dense retrievers increasingly claim the ability to condition retrieval on natural-language relevance criteria beyond a short query. We identify a specific weakness in this capability: the evaluated models perform well when lexical or attribute cues distinguish the target, but become much less reliable when relevance depends on a directional relation over the same entities. We introduce SwitchIR, a controlled diagnostic that contrasts Constraint Shift and Explicit Filtering with Relational Flip, where the query and document pair remain fixed while the requested relation direction reverses. Across representative embedding and late-interaction retrievers, the primary joint metric is high on the two cue-available controls and consistently low on Relational Flip; the same ordering persists in an LLM-assisted, manually reviewed realistic variant. An exact margin decomposition shows that both directions are correct only when the direction-sensitive query--document interaction exceeds the direction-invariant pair preference. Empirically, direction-sensitive changes are common, but their aligned interaction is usually too weak to overcome that preference. Diagnostic fine-tuning shows that models can learn relational behavior that generalizes across relation families, with stronger generalization in the larger model.

### 2. Faithful in Steps: Improving Generalization and Citation in RAG via Query Decomposition

- **LLM Score**: 0
- **Keyword Score**: 11
- **Authors**: Yue Liu, Zhongying Ru, Shimin Di, Jipeng Zhang, Ruiyuan Zhang, Xiaofang Zhou
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15538v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-11-11 14:22:09
- **Comment**: Accepted by AAAI 2026
- **Topic Keywords**: query, rerank, rag, retrieval, rank
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> Retrieval-augment generation is a prevalent strategy to mitigate hallucinations of LLMs. The attributable RAG (RAGQ) generates quotes for its answers. The quotes indicate which input contexts support the RAG to derive the answers, enhancing the answer's verifiability and trustworthiness. However, existing RAGQs exhibit significant degradation when dealing with questions that require multi-hop reasoning and multi-modal understanding, suffering from over-citation, implicit entity identification failure, and poor generalization. In this paper, we propose a novel RAGQ framework, namely QDRAG. QDRAG breaks down the input question into atomic subquestions to identify the implicit entities. Then, the reranker prunes context distractors to eliminate the downstream over-citation. To facilitate query decomposition, we propose two zero-shot approaches: QD-C and QD-R, which guide the QD MLLM to decompose the question based on context knowledge and retrieval rewards, respectively. One interesting finding is that finetuning on the QD task shows better generalizability compared to directly finetuning on the downstream RAGQ task. Experiments on four multi-modal QA benchmarks demonstrate QDRAG's efficacy in grounding answers and generating faithful citations. The framework significantly outperforms all the baselines on both in-domain and out-of-domain tests, even surpassing Gemini-Pro.

### 3. ReAlign: Reward-Guided Preference Alignment for Generative Re-Ranking

- **LLM Score**: 0
- **Keyword Score**: 11
- **Authors**: YaChen Yan, Liubo Li
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15445v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-11-11 13:16:18
- **Comment**: 9 pages, 3 figures, ACM conference format
- **Topic Keywords**: ranking, listwise, relevance, rank, search
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> Re-ranking refines the upstream ranked list to maximize listwise user utility. Existing two-stage generator-evaluator methods decouple list generation from list evaluation, leaving the generator without direct access to the listwise utility signal, while scoring-based one-stage methods score items under a fixed upstream permutation context without explicitly searching the permutation space. We propose \textbf{ReAlign}, a reward-guided preference alignment framework that couples a listwise reward model with a candidate-aware autoregressive generator. The reward model decomposes the listwise reward into position-wise contributions, modeling user exposure through a survival-based exposure module and item value through per-objective heads for heterogeneous engagement signals. The generator is trained in two stages: supervised pre-training on logged exposure lists to establish a relevance prior, followed by reward-guided post-training that aligns the generator with the reward model through complementary distribution and ranking alignment over within-pool standardized rewards. Extensive offline experiments show that ReAlign consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art baselines.

### 5. One Fabrication Is Enough: A Fake News Article Changes the Forecasts of LLM Forecasting Systems

- **LLM Score**: 0
- **Keyword Score**: 11
- **Authors**: Zeyu Zhang, Bradly C. Stadie
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15102v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-11-11 06:29:13
- **Comment**: 18 pages, 2 figures, 13 tables
- **Topic Keywords**: retriever, queries, rag, retrieval, search
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> LLMs are increasingly used for forecasting, and the best forecasting systems retrieve news as evidence to strengthen their predictions. This also opens them to attack. We study the weakest attacker of this kind, who knows only the forecasting question and publishes fabricated news articles, with no access to the model, the retriever or its queries. We evaluate how far this attack reaches on stock questions and real prediction-market questions. Once the article is in the model's context, it reverses 74% to 82% of the forecasts that opposed it, on every frontier model we tested. The model cannot verify the fabrication and follows it anyway. Retrieval is the attack's entry point, and the systems built to retrieve well are the most exposed: the architectures of two leading deployed forecasting systems, whose agents search for every related article, retrieve the fabricated one in about 90% of runs. Accuracy cannot reveal this harm: the attacked forecaster is right when the attacker's direction turns out right and wrong otherwise, so its average accuracy barely changes while its forecasts follow the attacker's guess. We also propose a second attacker, who publishes two articles that argue opposite directions to make the forecaster unsure. The model does not hedge; it returns a confident forecast in a direction the attacker no longer controls. We then evaluate the two strongest published retrieval-time defenses, passage filtering and partitioned retrieval. Each stops the one-article attack but is bypassed by a one-prompt change to the attack. Together they stop both bypasses at four articles, but sixteen distorted copies beat them, and they change 29% of clean decisions and discard 39% of the genuine news. Partitioned retrieval also requires control of the retrieval index. Systems that search the open web have no such control and are left with the filter alone. For them, the attack still works.

#### Abstract

#### Abstract
> Re-ranking is a crucial stage in recommender systems, yet existing evaluators oversimplify user browsing assumptions and collapse multi-objective signals into a single scalar, limiting their ability to reflect true listwise utility. We propose \textbf{SurvEval}, a listwise evaluator that jointly models user exposure and item value for re-ranking evaluation. We formalize the sequential browsing process as a discrete-time survival model, deriving monotonically decreasing reaching probabilities that capture the cascading structure of user browsing. #### Abstract
> LLM workloads sometimes ask many independent questions about the same long document, as in document QA, data labeling and LLM-based evaluation.

### 1. Jev-as-a-Judge for Brazilian Portuguese Information Retrieval: Relevance Assessment and Reranking

- **LLM Score**: 0
- **Keyword Score**: 21
- **Authors**: Leandro Carísio Fernandes, Jayr Pereira
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23346v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-10-16 11:52:46
- **Topic Keywords**: information retrieval, query, queries, ranking, rerank, relevance, retrieval, rank
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### T.A.R.G.E.T. Summary (from Full Text)
- **Topic**: Jev lightweight LLM for relevance judgments in Brazilian Portuguese IR
- **Aim**: Assess Jev’s agreement with reference qrels, ranking preservation, and reranking effectiveness on BM25
- **Rationale**: Generative LLMs require many inferences per pair; Jev offers single inference with typed decisions, reducing compute cost and latency
- **Ground**: Datasets: Quati, JurisTCU, NormasTCU, Ulysses‑RFCorpus; methods: typed‑choice prompts, structured JSON outputs, confusion matrices, quadratic‑weighted κ, Kendall’s τ for ranking comparison
- **Experiment**: 1) Relevance assessment: Jev‑as‑a‑Judge on 4 collections, compute exact‑match %, κ, disagreement distribution; 2) Reranking: pointwise Jev on top‑1000 BM25 candidates for 50 Quati queries, evaluate nDCG@10 and MRR; 3) Ranking preservation: recompute nDCG@10 and MRR for 31 systems using Jev‑generated qrels, compare rankings via Kendall’s τ
- **Takeaway**: Jev achieves ≥94.5 % within‑one‑level agreement, τ > 0.90 preserving system order, and boosts Quati nDCG@10 by 90 % and MRR by 32 %; it is two orders of magnitude cheaper than frontier generative models and enables scalable, multilingual IR evaluation

#### Abstract
> This study investigates Jev, a System One model designed for fast, structured decision-making, in information retrieval. We use Jev-as-a-judge to generate relevance judgments for four Brazilian Portuguese IR collections covering different domains and compare them with the official relevance judgments of each collection. We also evaluate Jev as a reranker. For relevance assessment, exact agreement ranged from 52.9% to 64.9%, while quadratic weighted Cohen's kappa ranged from 0.52 to 0.82. Most disagreements involved adjacent relevance levels, with at least 94.5% of annotations matching exactly or differing by only one relevance level. Despite differences at the individual query--document level, Jev-generated qrels produced similar retrieval system rankings, with Kendall's tau ranging from 0.90 to 0.93 for NormasTCU and Ulysses-RFCorpus across the two evaluation metrics. For reranking, Jev reordered the top 1,000 BM25 candidates for each of the 50 Quati test queries, increasing nDCG@10 from 0.3329 to 0.6331 (+90.2%) and MRR from 0.6852 to 0.9033 (+31.8%). These findings support Jev as a relevance assessor and show improved ranking effectiveness over BM25 in the evaluated reranking setting.

#### Abstract
> Consumers often describe what they need rather than use the terminology found on a banking website. For example, a natural-language request ("a card for travel") may not share the keywords of the relevant product page, and the problem is harder in bilingual services, where the same intent may be expressed in Arabic or English. This paper presents an evidence-gated bilingual retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) framework for consumer banking search that operates entirely on local infrastructure. #### Abstract
> As assistants move from reactive answering toward proactive intervention, memory changes role: it no longer only supplies the answer to a query, it determines whether and when to speak at all. Prior work in this setting hands the system the relevant fact in context; we study the regime that makes the problem hard, where the evidence justifying an intervention must be mined from a long-horizon personal memory. #### Abstract
> Strong multi-hop retrieval should be practical to deploy without expensive corpus preparation or complex database infrastructure. We present SAG2, an embedding-free retrieval architecture built around a single SQLite database, BM25, and contextual evidence decisions from JEV. SAG2 follows the query-time association principle of SAG (SQL-Retrieval Augmented Generation), while replacing its event-entity and dense indexes with a lexical index over original passages. Selected passages supply terms for further retrieval, and newly retrieved evidence guides subsequent decisions. Indexing all 11,656 MuSiQue passages takes 2.36 seconds, produces a 25.87 MiB database, and requires no model calls. SAG2 demonstrates that accurate multi-hop evidence retrieval can be supported by compact, rapidly prepared infrastructure, making it a practical foundation for knowledge retrieval applications.

- **Topic Keywords**: queries, rag, retrieval, search
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> Large language models (LLMs) can generate fluent outputs that lack source support or conflict with local operational constraints. We introduce Self-Reflective Chain-of-Thought (SRCoT), a modular architecture that organizes generation as a closed Retrieve--Reflect--Revise loop. SRCoT separates environment state, persistent knowledge, accumulated experience, and short-term memory into provenance-labeled stores. BM25 retrieval supplies local context; subsequent passes expose intermediate drafts, missing-information markers, follow-up queries, and optional claim--evidence mappings. These artifacts inform retrieval, revision, clarification, or abstention decisions. We examine the architecture through qualitative, trace-based case studies in two fully synthetic settings: emergency medical services and Munich-Sim, an emergency-operations simulation. Local prototype runs on an Apple M4-family laptop with 128 GB memory illustrate source-labeled documentation, explicit gap marking, and structured dispatch-style outputs. The traces also reveal provenance-assignment errors, incomplete evidence coverage, and unsafe assumptions that remain possible despite prompting constraints. These cases demonstrate report-level inspectability and local execution, not comparative superiority, hallucination reduction, clinical validity, or operational safety. The architecture separates retrieval and control from the model interface, but behavior across different models remains to be evaluated. SRCoT is a research proof of concept requiring human review, with its principal contribution lying in an inspectable control procedure rather than a new model.

### 7. Reasoning Reranking with Semantic IDs as the Sole Item Representation

- **LLM Score**: 0
- **Keyword Score**: 8
- **Authors**: Zhenwei Tang, Jintao Liu, Di Bai, Xidong Wu, Xinwu Cheng
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22797v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-10-16 01:21:47
- **Comment**: 30 pages, 3 figures. Code: https://github.com/lilv98/ReRankID ; data: https://huggingface.co/datasets/lilvjosephtang/ReRankID
- **Topic Keywords**: ranking, rerank, recommend, rank
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> Reasoning rerankers write a rationale before ranking a candidate slate, and existing systems read item metadata to do so, even though generative recommenders increasingly represent items as compact Semantic IDs (SIDs). Ranking from SIDs alone has two appeals: SID prompts are far shorter than the matched item text, and although item codes are fixed from content, the backbone learns their token embeddings in part from user-item interaction sequences, so the tokens can carry behavioral signal that item text lacks. That training targets next-item generation, and whether it supports ranking a supplied slate from SIDs alone is untested. We build ReRankID, a controlled testbed in which models reason and rank from a history and slate shown only as SIDs, varying item rendering, history length, and slate size one at a time. On this testbed, such models often copy the input order instead of reranking, and readable metadata does not repair the failure. We compare supervised fine-tuning, rejection-sampled self-training, on-policy distillation, and verifier-based reinforcement learning as post-training methods and find that they elicit the missing behavior. The strongest post-trained model, the 8B model trained with a graded ranking reward, gives the best SID-only ranking and matches strong text-reading LLMs whose prompts are about 20x longer. It ranks above random with no history at all, where a caption-reading reference does not, and it keeps its gains at long histories, where text prompts pass 50k tokens and the caption-reading reference stops improving. In a paired rationale audit, this model argues trade-offs better than that reference but describes items less accurately. The common post-training methods we test thus elicit ranking from SIDs but not faithful item descriptions, which calls for post-training that improves the reasoning itself, not only the ranking it produces.

### 16. First Passage Attention

- **LLM Score**: 0
- **Keyword Score**: 5
- **Authors**: Yuxuan Liu
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22714v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-10-15 22:31:45
- **Topic Keywords**: query, retrieval
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> Augmenting softmax attention with forget gates typically relies on summing decay contributions along the key--query path. Because such accumulation is monotonic, these mechanisms cannot selectively erase intermediate context while preserving older, structurally relevant tokens. We propose First Passage Attention (FPA), which shifts from path summation to path extrema by lowering a key's logit proportionally to the deepest dip of a learned scalar field below that key's level over the intervening span. FPA encompasses the Forgetting Transformer (FoX) as a special case, and when the field and levels follow a shared learned walk, the alive keys with unpenalised logits naturally form a stack. We show that a constant-dimension FPA head realises any subset of keys left alive by subsequent dips, whereas softmax heads with positional biases or token-level forget gates require a head dimension linear in the number of keys. In synthetic experiments, one-layer FPA heads converge to these target sets while comparable softmax and FoX baselines fail on every seed. Scaling to a 110M-parameter language model, FPA matches FoX in perplexity and uniquely achieves perfect passkey retrieval at $8\times$ the training length, even with its key--value cache compressed to about $25\%$.

#### T.A.R.G.E.T. Summary (from Full Text)
- **Topic**: MIPR – Multimodal Intent‑to‑Product Retrieval benchmark
- **Aim**: Evaluate retrieval systems that infer the best product(s) from implicit multimodal queries, testing reasoning over image+text, multi‑category solution coverage, and fine‑grained relevance.
- **Rationale**: Real Chinese e‑commerce search logs reveal implicit needs and constraints; a nuanced five‑level relevance scheme and diverse product categories provide a rigorous test for multimodal reasoning and ranking.
- **Ground**: Dataset: 3,522 Chinese multimodal queries, 30,731 unique Taobao listings, 447,809 relevance judgments. Five‑level labels: L1 3.92%, L2 5.97%, N1 2.20%, N2 20.81%, N3 67.10%. Avg. 4.99 L1 products/query, 3.16 solution categories/query, 75.6% queries with ≥2 categories, 51.8% with ≥3 categories, 2,782 leaf categories represented.
- **Experiment**: Construction pipeline: (1) Query curation via regex, LLM filtering (Qwen3.5‑9B, Claude Sonnet 4.6), dual‑LLM validation, human check (96.5% accuracy), redundancy removal. (2) Candidate product discovery using Qwen3.6‑27B term generation, multi‑route retrieval (BM25, BGE‑small‑zh‑v1.5, Qwen3‑Embedding‑0.6B, Qwen3‑VL‑Embedding‑2B). (3) Human validation on 200 samples.
- **Takeaway**: MIPR offers a large‑scale, zero‑shot benchmark that forces models to combine visual, textual, and contextual cues, evaluate breadth across product families, and leverage fine‑grained relevance signals, thereby advancing multimodal intent‑to‑product retrieval research.

#### Abstract
> Real-world e-commerce queries often express user intent without naming a target product, and a single intent may be fulfilled by products from multiple categories. However, existing multimodal retrieval benchmarks typically assume a relatively well-defined target, leaving retrieval for intents with multi-category solutions less explored. To fill this gap, we introduce MIPR, a benchmark for Multimodal Intent-to-Product Retrieval comprising 3,522 multimodal queries and a shared corpus of 30,731 products. MIPR features reasoning-intensive retrieval, multi-category solutions, and fine-grained relevance labels. Experiments show that the best embedding-based retriever achieves 49.23% nDCG@10 on the full query set, and 55.86% solution-category recall@10 on queries with multiple solution categories. Reranking improves these scores to 60.11% and 67.00%, respectively, yet substantial gaps to the candidate-pool oracle remain. These results establish MIPR as a challenging and diagnostic benchmark for evaluating multimodal intent-to-product retrieval. The data and code are publicly available at https://huggingface.co/datasets/leequeue/MIPR.

#### Abstract
> Conversational meme retrieval supports response selection in online communication, where relevance depends on both contextual meaning and affective expression. We study this task using two language-specific collections from Douban and Imgflip, containing 98,506 meme records across 5,583 discussion topics. Thread membership supplies weak relevance labels, while likes provide implicit ranking feedback. Automatically extracted sentiment, facial-expression indicators, and cross-modal consistency features support an exploratory analysis of engagement. We propose CARE, a Conversational Affective Response Retrieval framework that combines an emotion-enhanced CLIP retriever with a Jina-based reranker. They do not independently establish perceived social resonance or isolate the contribution of affect from reranker training. The study provides a basis for further evaluation of affect-aware retrieval with human relevance judgments and controlled comparisons.

### 3. Deal With It: On Better Schedules for Listwise Reranking

- **LLM Score**: 0
- **Keyword Score**: 18
- **Authors**: Benjamin Clavié, Herumb Shandilya, Hanna Lichtenberg, Aamir Shakir, Makoto P. Kato
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22392v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-11-17 08:42:11
- **Topic Keywords**: retriever, queries, ranking, rerank, listwise, rag, rank
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> Listwise LLM rerankers cannot reliably rank hundreds of documents in a single call, and rely on schedules to orchestrate their calls. Parallel schedules split candidates into groups and advance a fixed quota from each. When a group holds more of the top passages than its quota, some are cut, and expensive repair calls become required. We show that the way candidates are split matters where such a quota can bind, and that, in expected cuts, the best split there deals them round-robin by retriever rank, like cards. Although not conceptually new, dealing has remained unanalyzed. We prove that, under a simple retriever model, no split with equal quotas cuts fewer of the true top ten on average, and that contiguous chunks cut more as the retriever improves. DealRank, the simplest schedule our analysis allows, is on average within 0.1 nDCG@10 points of the best schedules over six LLMs, in two rounds. It is consistently 1.1 to 1.4 times faster than BlitzRank with concurrent first calls up to a thousand candidates. There, it needs three rounds and is eleven times faster than the sliding window. In existing schedules such as BlitzRank, dealing reduces repair calls from 59% of queries to 14%, improving latency with no significant change in quality. Random partitioning appears equivalent at a quota of four, as predicted, but ties results to the seed and trails by 1.3 nDCG@10 points at a quota of one over a dense retriever.

### 4. NONSENSE: Non-Semantic Strings Expose Semanticity Gaps in Dense Retrieval

- **Authors**: Ankit Gupta
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22353v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-11-17 08:04:40
- **Comment**: Accepted to EACL 2027 (Main Conference). 15 pages
- **Topic Keywords**: retriever, dense retrieval, query, relevance, retrieval, rank, search
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> Dense retrieval is central to modern search, question answering, and retrieval-augmented generation, where high similarity is often treated as evidence of semantic relevance. We examine how far this assumption can be stressed. NONSENSE is a black-box sparse-edit probe that constructs visibly non-semantic strings while forbidding query-term copying, optimizing only the dense similarity between a query and the string. Across five BEIR-style datasets and three popular single-vector retrievers, these strings frequently outscore the retriever's own best judged query-relevant passage (QRP), and often rank first in the full corpus. The result indicates a semanticity gap in the evaluated dense retrievers: nearest-neighbor status can be separated from passage-like meaning. These findings highlight the importance of evaluating dense retrievers not only by whether they retrieve relevant documents, but also by how reliably their similarity scores reflect semantic evidence.

### 5. IntentGR: Enhancing Intent for End-to-End Personalized Generative Retrieval in Search

- **Comment**: 11 pages, 3 figures; Accepted by WSDM 2027 Findings
- **Topic Keywords**: query, queries, rag, retrieval, recommend, commerce, e-commerce, search
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> Personalized generative retrieval in search should jointly satisfy the user's immediate search intent and long-term preference. Recent end-to-end approaches, primarily explored in recommendation, unify item tokenization and sequence generation within a single model, allowing semantic identifiers (SIDs) to be optimized toward the downstream generation objective. However, these methods often treat the query merely as an input feature, without explicitly leveraging it to guide either identifier learning or sequence generation. This leaves intent guidance weak in both the tokenization and the generation stage, causing generated semantic identifiers to drift toward high-frequency token patterns rather than the current search intent. In this work, we find that incorporating additional query-related evidence into the framework of end-to-end generative retrieval is important. Based on this finding, we propose IntentGR, an intent-enhanced end-to-end framework that strengthens query semantics at four complementary stages of the retrieval pipeline. Query-Aware Semantic Behavior Representation (QBR) introduces query-level behavioral evidence as a new input signal; Query-Semantic Alignment (QSA), Long-Tail Query Enrichment (LQE), and Query-conditioned Attention Modulation (QAM) then serve as integration mechanisms at the optimization, training-distribution, and decoding stages to fully exploit this evidence. Experiments on the public AOL4PS benchmark and AE, a large-scale industrial e-commerce dataset, show that IntentGR improves Recall@10 by 8.11% and 11.96% over the strongest baseline, with strong gains on long-tail queries. These results suggest that enriched query-related input evidence, together with effective integration mechanisms, is beneficial for building intent-aware end-to-end generative search systems.

#### Abstract
> Agentic search demands that a search engine move beyond per document relevance ranking to assemble document sets optimized for downstream LLM reasoning. Prior work addresses this either by improving individual pipeline stages in isolation or by applying RL based selection on top of relevance trained upstream modules, leaving the full stack unaligned to utility for reasoning. We propose rubric aligned search, a framework that unifies the traditional search pipeline under a single supervision surface. #### Abstract
> Agents built on large language models (LLMs) increasingly forecast real-world events by searching the Web and reasoning over what they find.

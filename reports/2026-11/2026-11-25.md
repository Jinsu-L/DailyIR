### 1. SOARS: System One Assisted Reranking for Agentic Search

- **LLM Score**: 0

- **Authors**: Amin Bigdeli, Liye Fu, Fattane Zarrinkalam, Joel Stremmel, Elizabeth Lingg
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32917v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-11-24 16:32:43
- **Topic Keywords**: query, ranking, rerank, relevance, rag, retrieval, rank, search

#### T.A.R.G.E.T. Summary (from Full Text)
- **Topic**: SOARS (System One Assisted Reranking for Agentic Search)
- **Aim**: Provide a lightweight, training‑free reranking pipeline that plugs a pre‑trained System One model into deep‑search agents, enabling efficient, high‑quality retrieval at each search step.
- **Rationale**: Existing LLM or cross‑encoder rerankers are invoked at every step, causing high latency, cost, and limited context (they only see the sub‑query). Retrieval quality suffers because the first‑stage retriever’s token window misses relevant passages. SOARS addresses these by composing the original question with the sub‑query, extracting concise, context‑rich snippets, and using a calibrated ordinal relevance signal from a System One model, all without additional training.
- **Ground**: Core design: 1) Query construction: τq_t = Q ⊕ q_t to capture global intent and local focus. 2) Document representation: extract earliest non‑stopword term from q_t, take 128‑token span, prepend title to form τd. 3) Reranking: feed (q_t, d) pairs to System One (e.g., Jev) to obtain calibrated probabilities over four relevance levels; compute expected relevance score and sort. Integration: sits after BM25 top‑k retrieval in the ReAct loop, implemented in Pi‑Serini, compatible with any LLM policy and first‑stage retriever.
- **Experiment**: Datasets: BrowseComp‑Plus (830 Qs, 100K docs) and WebShaper (200 Qs, 1M docs). Retriever: BM25 (k1=25, b=1, top‑k=100). Reranker: Jev (no fine‑tuning). Baselines: monoT5, gpt‑oss‑120b, RankGPT, Qwen3‑Reranker‑4B, GTE‑ModernBERT. Metrics: Retrieval R@5/R@10, agent accuracy, calibration error, recall measures; Efficiency: search calls, latency, cost. Key results: SOARS (composed query) achieves R@5 42.9, R@10 44.0 with 38.9 ms latency, matching or exceeding all LLM rerankers while being 10–100× faster. Agent accuracy on BrowseComp‑Plus jumps from 73.9 % to 84.7 % with fewer search calls (23→14).
- **Takeaway**: SOARS demonstrates that a single, training‑free System One reranking call per search step can replace expensive LLM rerankers, delivering higher retrieval recall, improved agent accuracy, reduced latency, and lower cost. Its evidence‑centric design, compatibility with any LLM policy and retriever, and scalability make sophisticated multi‑hop search agents practical for real‑world, resource‑constrained deployments.

#### Abstract
> Deep search agents address complex information needs through iterative retrieval and reasoning, where the relevance of retrieved evidence influences downstream performance. Existing rerankers improve retrieval effectiveness but introduce substantial latency and cost when invoked throughout the search trajectory. We introduce SOARS, a training-free framework leveraging a System One model for fast, calibrated relevance estimation. At each search step, SOARS selects query-relevant passages from long documents and reranks candidates in a single call, jointly considering the original question and agent-generated sub-query. Across six LLM backbones on BrowseComp-Plus and WebShaper, SOARS consistently improves answer accuracy and evidence recall while reducing search calls, end-to-end latency, and inference cost. On BrowseComp-Plus, it improves GPT-5.4 Mini's accuracy from 73.9% to 84.7% while reducing search calls from 23 to 14, demonstrating the potential of System One reranking for more effective and efficient deep search.

#### Abstract
> Multimodal retrieval must match dense pages, fine-grained entities, ordered video, and agent tools and memory. Single-vector embeddings lose local evidence, cross-encoders reread every recalled item online, and uncompressed late interaction is too large to serve. We present OmniEVIE, a 4.5B multi-vector embedding model covering text, images, visual documents, video, and tools with one compressed index and a three-stage retriever. Each record stores at most 128 int8 vectors of dimension 64 (9,472 bytes, or 8.82 GiB per million records), providing a mean vector, 1-bit signs, and int8 multi-vectors for coarse, middle, and final search while localizing each hit to a page region or video interval. On 1M and 5M records, a single-thread query takes 54.2 ms and 120.2 ms (11.1x and 25.3x faster than exact 4096-d single-vector search; 236x and 532x faster than full int8 MaxSim). Video is encoded directly from frames without a parsing model: on QVHighlights (1,083 queries), the peak falls inside the labeled segment for 78.0% of queries and the returned span (18% of video length) intersects it for 94.3% vs. 44.9% random. Weights and code: https://huggingface.co/tencent/OmniEVIE-4B and https://github.com/Tencent/OmniEVIE.

- **Topic**: Tencent Yuanbao AI Search – LARF: Learning‑Aligned Reasoning Framework for Search Relevance
- **Aim**: Enhance query‑document relevance estimation in AI‑native search by overcoming limited supervision, lack of margin separation in RL objectives, and latency constraints of chain‑of‑thought reasoning.
- **Rationale**: Large language models lack domain‑specific supervision, trajectory‑level RL rewards fail to enforce margin between adjacent ordinal relevance labels, and chain‑of‑thought reasoning is too slow for online ranking.
- **Ground**: A three‑stage pipeline: 1) Cold‑start supervised fine‑tuning with multi‑agent guided chain‑of‑thought to generate richer rationales; 2) Discriminative GRPO with attribute‑wise, list‑wise, and rule‑consistency rewards plus a margin loss on the terminal relevance token; 3) Dual‑head student distillation that retains classification heads for non‑autoregressive, latency‑efficient inference.

#### Abstract
> Query-document relevance estimation is a core component of AI-native search systems, where a model must assess whether retrieved documents satisfy users' information needs under complex, multi-dimensional criteria. However, directly applying large language models (LLMs) to this task remains challenging because conventional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) provides limited supervision for domain-specific judgment rationales, while standard trajectory-level reinforcement learning (RL) objectives do not explicitly enforce margin separation among adjacent ordinal relevance labels. Moreover, although such reasoning can be useful during training, chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning is impractical for latency-sensitive online ranking. These challenges make it difficult to resolve ambiguous decision boundaries. We propose a three-stage framework LARF (Learning-Aligned Reasoning Framework for Search Relevance): it first performs cold-start SFT on strategically selected samples with multi-agent guided CoT rationales, then applies discriminative GRPO with attribute-wise, list-wise, and rule-consistency rewards together with a margin loss on the terminal relevance token, and finally distills the teacher into a dual-head student whose classification heads are retained for non-autoregressive inference. On a human-annotated web search relevance benchmark with approximately 300K training and 16K test instances, LARF-8B improves six-class classification accuracy by 5.78 percentage points and NDCG by 5.05 points, while the classification-only serving path reduces inference latency by 82.9\%. These results indicate that aligning structured reasoning, terminal decision optimization, and classification-head distillation can provide an effective accuracy--efficiency trade-off for industrial search relevance modeling.

### 4. See the Answer, Learn to Embed: Learning Universal Multimodal Embeddings from Hindsight

- **LLM Score**: 0
- **Keyword Score**: 9
- **Authors**: Yoonki Cho, Youngju Na, Taegeun Yang, Jinhwan Seo, Sung-Eui Yoon
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32439v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-11-24 11:39:03
- **Topic Keywords**: query, queries, pairwise
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> Universal Multimodal Embedding (UME) models map heterogeneous multimodal inputs into a shared embedding space, where pairwise similarity supports diverse downstream tasks. Recent reasoning-driven UME approaches use intermediate reasoning to generate additional context for embedding, but often incur extra computation at inference. In this paper, we explore the matched candidate (i.e., the paired target) as an alternative source of context, asking whether a model can "see the answer" during training and "learn to embed" without it at inference. However, direct conditioning on this privileged context can introduce candidate-specific information unavailable from the query alone or cause the candidate-conditioned query representation to collapse toward the candidate embedding. To address this, we introduce Hindsight-Enhanced Multimodal Embeddings (HEME), which learns query-grounded hindsight representations from such candidate context. HEME first trains a hindsight teacher with a hindsight attention mask, mismatched-candidate conditioning, and query-token alignment to limit excessive reliance on the candidate. It then freezes the teacher and distills its hindsight representation and similarity distribution into a standalone student embedder. At inference, the student independently encodes queries and candidates without candidate conditioning or the hindsight teacher. Experiments on MMEB-V2 and the agent and text tasks of MMEB-V3 demonstrate the effectiveness of HEME and show that it enables more efficient inference than explicit reasoning-driven methods.

#### T.A.R.G.E.T. Summary (from Full Text)
- **Topic**: Reliable uncertainty quantification for large‑language‑model based recommendation systems
- **Aim**: To assess and compare diverse UQ signals for detecting ranking failures (e.g., target item missing from top‑10) in a controlled, record‑level setting
- **Rationale**: Ranking outputs depend on prompts, decoding, and context, making UQ signals heterogeneous and hard to compare; fixing the ranking isolates monitoring quality and allows fair comparison of UQ methods
- **Ground**: Eight heterogeneous UQ signals evaluated as record‑level monitors; base rankings of 20 candidates generated once per record; experiments conducted with Llama‑3.1‑8B‑Instruct across four datasets and four settings (warm‑start, sparse‑history, item‑text robustness, tail‑target); Qwen‑3‑8B evaluated on the same 4,000 reference instances for cross‑model consistency
- **Experiment**: Fixed‑ranking evaluation design mapping all UQ signals to a common record‑level output; evaluation task of ranking‑failure detection (flagging Error@10 cases); second complementary task implied but not fully described
- **Takeaway**: A fixed‑ranking, record‑level evaluation framework enables fair comparison of UQ signals for ranking failure detection; cross‑model consistency testing highlights robustness; reliable UQ is essential for trustworthy LLM‑based recommendation systems

#### Abstract
> Large language models (LLMs) provide a flexible interface for recommendation systems. However, their issued rankings require reliable instance-level uncertainty monitoring. In this scenario, comparing uncertainty quantification (UQ) methods becomes challenging because LLM rankers lack a standard item-classification head. Their scores may depend on option labels, prompts, generation procedures, and the available user and item evidence. Existing methods derive uncertainty from different sources and apply it at different stages of the recommendation process. Some methods evaluate an issued ranking, whereas others use uncertainty to change the ranking itself, making direct comparison difficult. Here we introduce a fixed-ranking benchmark which evaluates eight heterogeneous UQ signals as record-level monitors under a shared output contract. We evaluate these signals across four recommendation datasets and two LLM backbones under a shared fixed-ranking protocol. From the results, we draw three main observations. First, UQ method ordering changes across backbones even on the same reference instances. Second, ranking-aligned Top10Mass provides the strongest scalar Error@10 detection in several evaluation conditions, reflecting effective cutoff-aligned monitoring, while its advantage narrows for popularity-tail targets. Third, scalar UQ signals are weak at identifying which control has the larger clipped NDCG@10 difference, whereas source-specific signals, including Perturbation Rank Instability and simple listwise distances, provide stronger paired-control agreement, reflecting their sensitivity to the two controlled evidence sources. These findings motivate us to evaluate ranking-failure detection, robustness across recommendation conditions, and responses to controlled evidence changes as distinct properties of UQ.

#### Abstract
> Large language model (LLM) agents rely on external memory to retain and reuse information across interactions. Existing memory systems treat memory as a record of the past and evaluate it by fidelity to the conversation. However, the value of memory lies in whether it improves the agent's response. A memory system decides what to memorize and what to serve before that response exists, and fidelity checks neither decision against it. Faithful memory can therefore fail to improve the response. We instead view memory as a prediction problem and present \textbf{\emph{\leto{}}}, a memory system for LLM agents that focuses on the utility of memory to the future rather than on its fidelity to the past. In long-context understanding, it is on par with retrieval over the raw history while serving fewer context tokens.

### 12. Speculative Decoding for Semantic-ID-Based Generative Recommendations

- **LLM Score**: 0
- **Keyword Score**: 5
- **Authors**: Harshal A. Chaudhari
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32855v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-11-24 15:57:21
- **Comment**: 33 pages, 19 figures (23 pages main text, appendices A-E follow). Preprint
- **Topic Keywords**: retrieval, recommend, rank, search
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> Generative recommender systems with Semantic IDs (SIDs) represent each item as a short sequence of $L$ discrete tokens and use a sequence model to predict the next item's tokens from a user's history. Industry-scale deployments typically use these models to retrieve multiple candidates per request with constrained beam search. However, this incurs a fixed cost of $L$ sequential model calls per request, and accuracy degrades as the SID generator becomes less sharp, without a guarantee on how close the output is to the exact top-$K$. Production systems therefore cannot detect degraded retrieval for individual requests.
  We propose SIDECAR, which uses approximate draft candidates and a tree-attention pass to score the entire draft in a single model call, memory permitting. This pass bounds the probability of every unexplored SID prefix and certifies the exact top-$K$ if the draft contains them. Otherwise, a branch-and-bound fallback expands only the parts of the trie that can still change the output, reaching certification within at most $L$ calls for any draft. Moreover, under a budget of $M$ tokens per call and $Y$ calls, which for $Y = L$ matches the resources of a beam search of width $M$, SIDECAR announces a factor $1 + δ$ within which its list will be exact at every rank. Depending on model sharpness, SIDECAR returns the exact top-$K$ using a fraction of beam search's calls or memory. Even when the budget is too small to recover the exact top-$K$, SIDECAR, unlike beam search, reports the announced factor, allowing the serving system to stop early once a quality target or latency limit is reached.

#### Abstract
> Automated understanding of handwritten Arabic student essays remains challenging due to script complexity and high variability
  in children's writing. We introduce ArEssays, a large-scale dataset of authentic essays from primary to secondary students
  across Egypt, with both verbatim transcriptions and linguistically corrected versions. We benchmark 34 vision-language and OCR
  models, including fine-tuned systems, and find large performance gaps on real student handwriting. We release the dataset,
  training pipelines, and results to support research in Arabic OCR, document understanding, and educational NLP.

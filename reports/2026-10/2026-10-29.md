- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> Generative engine optimization needs a scorer that ranks candidate pages by the share of an answer they will receive as citations. We measure pretrained rerankers, a hosted typed decision model (Jev), eight open decision models and distilled students on GEO-Bench queries answered by ten engines that receive the same candidate sources in their prompt. Where several engines answer the same query, they agree with one another at a within-group Spearman correlation of 0.704. Jev and two open 27B decision models, used without training, are the strongest scorers at 0.575 to 0.578. A Qwen3-Reranker-4B fine-tuned with low-rank adapters on teacher labels for other GEO-Bench queries, released as GEO Citation Ranker 4B, reaches 0.542 to 0.549 across three seeds, and its differences with the 27B models and with Jev are not distinguishable from zero on 229 queries. Most of the remaining distance between the student and Jev disappears when Jev scores one passage at a time, as the student does. We release the model, the benchmark, the predictions of every reported scorer and the code.

#### Abstract
> Generative recommendation (GR) reformulates item retrieval as autoregressive generation of discrete semantic identifiers (SIDs), offering a unified paradigm for personalized recommendation. GR4AD, as an industrial generative recommendation framework, has greatly improved advertising revenue while enabling efficient real-time serving in Kuaishou.However, continued deployment has exposed three limitations: deterministic SID assignment remains sensitive to semantic boundaries; conventional backbone scaling provides diminishing revenue gains under strict serving constraints; and sequence-level value optimization provides limited credit assignment to intermediate SID decisions, causing likelihood-based beam search to prematurely prune potentially high-value candidates. ### 3. TCC-RAG: Temporal Causal Chain-Guided RAG for Temporal Knowledge Graph Question Answering

- **LLM Score**: 0
- **Keyword Score**: 9
- **Authors**: Xinxun Zhang, Yuxiang Wang, Shanshan Feng, Jun Zhang, Tieyun Qian, Binhang Yuan, Kai Yin, Xiao Yan, Jiawei Jiang
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38366v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-10-28 07:03:44
- **Comment**: 14 pages, 6 figures, ICDE2027 Research Track
- **Topic Keywords**: query, rag, retrieval augmented generation, retrieval
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) have spurred growing interest in temporal knowledge graph question answering (TKGQA). Most existing methods rely on semantic retrieval augmented generation (RAG), which suffers from fundamental limitations in complex temporal reasoning. On the one hand, semantic-based RAG methods struggle to capture the multi-hop causal knowledge required for complex temporal reasoning. On the other hand, these methods introduce excessive redundant knowledge, trapping LLMs in spurious reasoning chains that potentially result in erroneous answers. To address these problems, we propose a Temporal Causal Chain-guided RAG (TCC-RAG) to retrieve a concise yet sufficient set of causal knowledge to support complex temporal reasoning. Specifically, we reformulate the temporal knowledge graph into a multi-view causal cue graph, with causal cues inducing connections that explicitly encode temporal evolution and causal dynamics. Based on the causal cue graph, we construct a query-specific temporal causal chain to guide the retrieval process, enabling the model to focus on a concise set of causally relevant knowledge. Finally, we introduce a causal evidence filter to refine the retrieved knowledge, ensuring its causal consistency with the underlying temporal logic of the query. Experiments show that TCC-RAG achieves substantial performance gains of 10.72% and 8.40% on two TKGQA benchmarks, and delivers a remarkable 62.97% improvement for multi-step temporal reasoning.

### 5. CoWalk: Cooperative Walks for Agentic Batch Queries on Disk-Resident Graphs

- **LLM Score**: 0
- **Keyword Score**: 8
- **Authors**: Qiao Liang, Zhiguang Chen, Yue Yu, Nong Xiao
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38404v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-10-28 07:49:54
- **Topic Keywords**: query, queries, rank, search
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> Workloads such as query rewriting, deep research and group-relative policy optimization (GRPO) issue batches of semantically related vector queries. Existing disk-resident approximate nearest neighbor (ANN) systems process these queries independently. On three workloads, 31 to 67 percent of the I/Os of a batch fetch a page that another query of the batch has already fetched. A page cache shared by the batch removes these I/Os, yet it makes a single-thread search only $1.1$ to $1.2{\times}$ faster, because the search time goes to distance computation and the cache does not change the walk of any query. We present CoWalk, which runs the queries of a batch as cooperating walks on one thread. Every query keeps its own candidate list, a page is read once per batch, and the full-precision vector on every fetched page is ranked by every query of the batch. A query therefore reaches the target recall with a shorter candidate list, which computes fewer distances and issues fewer I/Os. At recall@10 of 0.95, CoWalk answers a batch on one thread $1.6$ to $1.9{\times}$ faster than the fastest per-query baseline and issues $2.8$ to $6.9{\times}$ fewer I/Os. On a server it serves $3.0$ to $6.9{\times}$ the throughput of that baseline at a similar batch latency.

#### Abstract
> Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly used as recommendation generators in conversational recommender systems (RS) via retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). This flexible design introduces new sources of structural bias. This paper studies positional bias in top-1 and single-turn LLM-based RS: systematic dependence of the recommended item on the order in which candidates are presented in the prompt. We propose two complementary detection methodologies: (i) POB-ICM, based on contingency matrix analysis and Chi-square independence testing, and (ii) POB-UNBP, a sample-efficient approach derived from a uniformity null model under permutation-based evaluation, enabling stochastic decoding with formal statistical guarantees. Using a controlled RAG testbed for product recommendation, built on a real product catalog, a fixed prompt template and 2000 template-generated user queries with 20 permutations each (40,000 instances), both methodologies reject the null hypothesis of positional independence and uniformity in all evaluated state-of-the-art models. We introduce three complementary metrics -- permutation sensitivity, positional bias, and strong positional bias -- to quantify this effect, the latter targeting cases where the position displaces the recommendation towards clearly less relevant items rather than only choosing among comparably relevant ones. A robustness analysis across candidate-set size and decoding temperature shows that the bias is structural and model-dependent, not an artifact of deterministic decoding or candidate-set size. These results establish positional bias as a measurable, reproducible property of prompt-mediated recommendation pipelines in the evaluated setting and motivate routine auditing and governance controls (e.g., order randomization, standardized prompting) for deployed LLM-based RS. Source code is publicly released.

#### Abstract
> When an LLM agent fails, repair methods choose which action to replace and what to put in its place. ### 9. Measured or Real? When Model-Derived Measurements Can Be Corrected or Compared

- **LLM Score**: 0
- **Keyword Score**: 6
- **Authors**: Yifeng Li, Ziang Ni
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38015v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-10-27 22:12:59
- **Topic Keywords**: ranking, rag, rank
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> Numbers computed from trained models, such as an example's difficulty or a model's benchmark lead, change with seed and prompt. We ask when such measurements can be corrected or compared. Noise shrinks correlations, and the textbook correction divides by the square root of two estimated reliabilities. On two CIFAR-100 difficulty scores, it returned correlations above one in 70 of 100 classes. We prove that its mean squared error is infinite even when the correlation and the reliabilities use separate seeds. Our rule corrects only when both reliabilities pass a significance test, then divides by their upper confidence bounds. Prompt settings can reverse rankings. On ARC, Qwen2-1.5B trails Llama-3.2-3B by 3.8 points with no examples in the prompt and leads by 16.6 with two. Across four benchmarks, averaging the two settings names one winner for 15 of 16 such reversals. We compare models within each setting, controlling family-wise error asymptotically in the number of items for a fixed prompt grid. Aligning embedding coordinates across seeds raises held-out per-dimension reliability from 0.003 to 0.813.

#### Abstract
> LLM-based generative recommendation reads long, text-rich interaction histories but generates only short semantic identifiers (SIDs). This asymmetry makes supervised fine-tuning inefficient: encoding the history is expensive, yet each history usually provides supervision for only one target. We present Merge and Branch (M&B) to reduce input cost and learn more from each encoded history. Merge compresses each historical item's SID and text into a small number of learned tokens. Branch uses the compressed history to predict multiple target SIDs. To share history computation while keeping these targets independent, M&B organizes each example as a shallow, wide tree. A tree-structured key/value cache extends history sharing to constrained decoding. Experiments on public benchmarks show that M&B outperforms strong recommendation baselines while substantially improving training and decoding efficiency.

#### Abstract
> Key-value (KV) caching is a core mechanism in large language models, where intermediate key and value representations are stored to accelerate autoregressive decoding. As generation length increases, KV cache memory and computation grow linearly, making compression critical for efficient long-output reasoning. Dictionary-based sparse KV compression is appealing because it represents each token's KV states as a sparse combination of learned basis elements (atoms) in a dictionary, preserving token-level structure without enforcing a global low-rank constraint. However, existing dictionary-based methods face two practical limitations during decoding: iterative sparse coding methods such as Orthogonal Matching Pursuit add decoding-time overhead, and static dictionaries may mismatch evolving KV distributions under test-time generation. While adaptive dictionary updates can reduce this mismatch, naive append-only strategies lead to dictionary growth during generation. We propose FAD-KV, a \textbf{F}ast \textbf{A}daptive \textbf{D}ictionary KV caching framework for efficient reasoning decoding. FAD-KV integrates (i) a screening-based sparse coding scheme, where a one-shot Top-\(n\) selection identifies a small candidate pool followed by lightweight coefficient refinement, and (ii) a fixed-size dictionary adaptation mechanism that reuses inactive atoms instead of expanding the dictionary. By controlling both sparse coding cost and adaptive dictionary growth, FAD-KV provides a memory-bounded and computation-efficient KV compression framework for long reasoning workloads. Experiments on reasoning and long-context benchmarks show that FAD-KV achieves a strong quality-efficiency trade-off while avoiding unbounded dictionary growth during decoding.

#### Abstract
> Language model assistants sometimes change a correct answer when the user suggests a wrong one. The behavior, called sycophancy, is usually attributed to preference-based post-training, yet early measurements found it in pretrained models. We ask where it comes from by measuring it at every available stage of training: 13 open checkpoints from 5 lineages, from base model through supervised fine-tuning and preference optimization to release. We separate how far an injected claim moves a model's answer from how firmly the answer was held, and vary who makes the claim, how reliable they are said to be, and how strongly they make it. Two sycophancy behaviors with different origins emerge, and both replicate across datasets. Deference to a claim in the prompt is already present in every base model, which ranks sources in the same order as its post-trained descendants. Capitulation in conversation, dropping a stated answer when the user objects, is largely absent from base models and present in every post-trained checkpoint. When the objection names an alternative, most post-trained models give up firmly held answers as often as loosely held ones. We discuss the implications of these measurements for evaluating sycophancy and approaches for addressing each behavior.

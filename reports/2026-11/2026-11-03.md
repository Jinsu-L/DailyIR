### 1. Re$^3$Rank: Listwise Reranking with Reasoning-Conditioned Retrieval Heads

- **LLM Score**: 0
- **Keyword Score**: 20
- **Authors**: Ruiyi Yan, Zhuoyuan Mao, Yugo Murawaki, Yiwen Guo
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02550v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-11-02 05:24:12
- **Topic Keywords**: query, ranking, rerank, listwise, relevance, rag, retrieval, rank
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### T.A.R.G.E.T. Summary (from Abstract)
- **Topic**: Re^3Rank: Reasoning-Conditioned Retrieval Head Reranker
- **Aim**: Improve listwise reranking by conditioning retrieval heads on a generated reasoning trace, enabling better evidence retrieval with modest model sizes.
- **Rationale**: Traditional attention-based rerankers rely on question-only queries, limiting their ability to locate relevant evidence. By generating a reasoning trace conditioned on both question and candidate passages, and using it as an attention query for RRHeads that output continuous relevance scores, the model can better capture relevance signals.
- **Ground**: The architecture includes a generator producing reasoning traces, a scorer using RRHeads, and a straight-through estimator for end-to-end training. The RRHeads read relevance as continuous scores rather than discrete attention masses.
- **Experiment**: Evaluated on 9 benchmarks, Re^3Rank outperforms 11 baseline rerankers. The 4B-parameter variant leads by 3.11 NDCG@10 on average, while the 0.6B variant surpasses rerankers with up to 32B parameters.
- **Takeaway**: Conditioning retrieval heads on a generated reasoning trace yields superior reranking performance, achieving state-of-the-art results even with small models.

#### Abstract
> Attention-based rerankers score each candidate by the attention mass that retrieval heads route to it from the question, but the question, serving as a fixed query, carries limited information for locating relevant evidence. We propose Re$^3$Rank, a listwise reranker built on reasoning-conditioned retrieval heads (RRHeads). A generator first writes a reasoning trace over the question and the candidates, and a scorer then uses the trace tokens as the attention query of a few selected RRHeads, reading relevance as continuous scores. We present a straight-through estimator to propagate the ranking loss through the discrete trace, so that the generator and the scorer can be trained jointly for ranking. Averaged over 9 benchmarks, Re$^3$Rank outperforms 11 baseline rerankers: our 4B variant exceeds the strongest baseline by 3.11 NDCG@10 points on average, and even our 0.6B variant surpasses rerankers with up to 32B parameters.

### 2. Every Clause Counts: Instruction-Following Reranking by Multiplying Small, Answerable Questions

- **LLM Score**: 0
- **Keyword Score**: 19
- **Authors**: S. M. Mahbub Uddin Ahmed, Abdullah Al Musaddiq
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03179v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-11-02 14:15:23
- **Topic Keywords**: query, queries, ranking, rerank, relevance, rag, rank, search
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> People often attach instructions to their search queries, asking for pages written for beginners, documents in a certain language or official sources only. Most rerankers were trained to judge topical relevance and treat such instructions as a few extra words. On the InfoSearch benchmark, even GPT-4o used as a list-wise reranker reaches an average WISE (Weighted Instruction Sensitivity Evaluation) of only 33.5. We present DMReranker (Decision-Model Reranker), a zero-shot reranker that splits an instructed query into a topic and a short list of typed clauses. A small judge model answers one narrow question per clause, and the answer probabilities are multiplied into a single score. Nothing is trained, and the configuration was frozen before the test runs. Reranking the same top-100 candidates as the published baselines, DMReranker reaches a macro WISE of 50.9 over all six InfoSearch dimensions while keeping original-mode nDCG@10 at 82.9. It trails GPT-4o on the stricter SICR (Strict Instruction Compliance Ratio) metric, 19.2 against 32.1. We show that this gap follows from how a product of probabilities behaves when SICR compares raw scores across runs. Computed from ranks, our SICR is 67.0. On the same judge, splitting the instruction raises WISE from 32.9 to 47.6 over a single combined question, while raw-score SICR falls. Scoring all six dimensions cost about $10 at list price.

#### Abstract

#### Abstract
> Relevance modeling is fundamental to e-commerce search, determining how well retrieved products satisfy the needs expressed in a user's query. Accurate judgments require interpreting user intent, comparing product attributes, and applying business-specific matching criteria. Current methods based on large language models (LLMs) leverage contextual understanding and reasoning, focusing on incorporating domain-specific context and enhancing reasoning performance. Despite these advances, difficult query-product pairs remain challenging when evidence from different perspectives supports conflicting conclusions. A single reasoning pass may fail to reconcile these conflicts and ensure that decisive evidence guides the final judgment. To address this challenge, we propose Collaborative Adjudication with Debate and Replay Experience (CADRE), a framework for evidence-based adjudication in e-commerce relevance assessment. CADRE organizes three expert agents sharing a frozen backbone to analyze product attributes, user intent, and business rules in separate contexts, while a trainable Verifier assesses their supporting evidence against the applicable matching criteria to determine relevance. Under expert disagreement, a Verifier-guided debate elicits targeted clarification and revision of disputed interpretations before final adjudication. We optimize the Verifier through multi-turn group relative policy optimization and adapt successful-experience replay so that trajectories yielding correct terminal labels can be reused alongside fresh rollouts in subsequent updates. For efficient online serving, we use CADRE as an offline teacher to distill its relevance judgments into a lightweight student model. Offline evaluations and online A/B tests on an e-commerce search advertising platform demonstrate that CADRE improves relevance classification and key business and relevance satisfaction metrics.

#### Abstract
> Long-term conversational memory faces a timing mismatch: write-time summaries and graphs organize history before a future query is known, while independent retrieval can allocate a limited evidence budget to redundant or fragmented turns. We introduce Chain on Demand (CoD), a query-time evidence organization framework over an unchanged turn-level memory. Given a broad candidate pool, CoD constructs a temporary evidence chain by allocating a fixed anchor budget according to query relevance, lexical diversity relative to selected anchors, and newly covered query terms, followed by deterministic restoration of each selected anchor's same-session neighborhood.

### 1. SkillScaffold: Scaffolding Agentic Reinforcement Learning with Policy-Adaptive Skill Credit Assignment and Selective Injection

- **LLM Score**: 0
- **Keyword Score**: 5
- **Authors**: Wenlin Zhong, Hao Wang, Kun Yuan, Fan Yang, Jinluan Yang, Dian Jin, Xu Tan, Yiquan Wu, Kun Kuang
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18807v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-10-13 17:46:25
- **Topic Keywords**: query, rag
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> Agentic reinforcement learning (RL) enables large language models (LLMs) to learn from multi-turn interactions with verifiable outcomes. Textual skills can improve exploration by distilling prior interactions into reusable guidance. However, existing skill-based RL approaches often either assume skill injection is beneficial or rely on indirect feedback or rollout-intensive evaluation to assess its utility. Our pilot studies show that rollout-based utility estimation for skill credit assignment is costly and unstable under limited sampling, while skill benefits vary across candidates and depend on the current policy's competence on each query. To address these challenges, we introduce SkillScaffold, a policy-adaptive training scaffold that combines low-cost skill-utility estimation with selective skill injection. By reusing outcome-verified training trajectories, SkillScaffold generates candidate skills and estimates their utility through outcome-aware likelihood comparisons, without candidate-specific environment rollouts. The resulting utility guides whether and which skill to inject, retaining skill-free execution when no candidate is estimated to help. Joint training enables the Generator to produce skills tailored to the evolving Executor. Scaffolded RL encourages skill internalization for skill-free inference. Experiments on ALFWorld, WebShop, ScienceWorld, and $τ^2$-Bench demonstrate gains over strong agentic-RL and skill-based RL baselines across both short- and long-horizon tasks. Ablations and further analyses support the importance of utility-based selection and abstention. Our code is available at https://github.com/Vaidurya00/SkillScaffold.

### 2. The Last Remote Sensing Model Built by Humans? Casting Any Earth Observation Task as Recursive Self-Improvement

- **LLM Score**: 0
- **Keyword Score**: 3
- **Authors**: Sijie Zhao, Zeqi Zhou, Heli Qi, Xuanchao Fu, Zhuo Zheng, Junshi Xia, Junjue Wang, Weihao Xuan, Naoto Yokoya
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18845v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-10-13 17:57:51
- **Comment**: Ongoing project
- **Topic Keywords**: ctr, search
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> Earth observation (EO) models are still built by hand. For each new task, experts design a training recipe and tune it against a benchmark. We show that this loop can be handed to an agent. Any verifiable task can be cast as a harness and a verifier, where the harness defines what the agent may use and the verifier defines what counts as success. Every EO task can be made verifiable, through labeled benchmarks, independent sensors, physical models or the repeated observation of the same place. Given only these two components, a general-purpose coding agent builds the EO model and improves it recursively on its own outputs. Settings long treated as separate research problems, from learning with a handful of labels to adapting across domains, become choices of harness and verifier. On four EO benchmarks spanning optical, SAR and multispectral time-series imagery, agent-built models surpass the state of the art and keep most of that accuracy from about 1% of the training labels. This establishes a new paradigm for EO, in which people design harnesses and verifiers and agents build the models, so EO models improve as agents do.

### 3. Back-Translation Still Matters: Leveraging Monolingual Data for Low-Resource Machine Translation with LLMs

- **LLM Score**: 0
- **Keyword Score**: 2
- **Authors**: Divyanshu Bathla, Parag Singla, Jitin Singla
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18818v2>
- **Submitted**: 2026-10-13 17:50:18
- **Topic Keywords**: rag
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> Pre-trained Large Language Model (LLMs) are effective on many tasks in zero-shot and few-shot settings. For machine translation of low-resource languages, the obvious options are few-shot prompting and fine-tuning on a small parallel set. We ask: what if moderate amounts of low-resource monolingual data are also available? We argue that, rather than continued pretraining, a more effective use of this data is the pre-LLM era technique of back-translation (BT). The current fine-tuned LLM translates monolingual sentences in each language, and the resulting pairs train it in the reverse direction. Updates on the parallel set are interleaved for stable learning. With 1K parallel pairs on Odia and Swahili, BT beats continued pretraining into the low-resource language by 7.7 to 16.4 XCOMET-XXL points in domain. With beam-decoded intermediate translations, it also beats a model with 10 time more parallel data. In-context examples retrieved from the parallel data further improve BT. We also analyze the source, quality and amount of monolingual text. Across five low-resource languages, with Hindi as a high-resource control, and two 3B backbones, BT improves translation into the low-resource language by 16 to 173% XCOMET-XXL over parallel-only fine-tuning. Blind human evaluation on Odia and Sanskrit agrees with the automatic metrics.

### 4. Where Do LLM Ensemble Gains Come From? Decomposing Candidate Value, Diversity, and Aggregation

- **LLM Score**: 0
- **Keyword Score**: 2
- **Authors**: Donghwan Kim
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18786v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-10-13 17:36:51
- **Comment**: 14 pages, 6 figures
- **Topic Keywords**: rag
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> LLM ensemble gains are often measured against a single baseline, but the value attributed to additional candidates can change with the comparison used. We study an LLM aggregator under four matched conditions: Direct (no candidates), Single-slot (one candidate in a multi-slot interface), Duplicate (one candidate repeated), and Multi-candidate (separately generated candidates). Under either non-diverse control, overall candidate gain (CIV) splits algebraically into an additional-candidate contrast (DSV) and a candidate-context contrast (CCV); the split, but not CIV, depends on the chosen control. On MultiHopRAG model diversity (k=2), the repetition gap Rep=-8.33 pp [-13.25, -3.75] quantifies the difference between gains measured against Duplicate and Single-slot. On a matched FRAMES stronger-panel extension (n=200, k=3, aggregator output limit 4096), paired Delta Rep=+9.67 pp [+6.00, +13.50]; within panels, Rep changes sign (original -6.33; stronger +3.33). In FRAMES evidence aggregation, candidate-context contrasts remain strongly negative under both non-diverse controls. Our results show why ensemble evaluations should state the reference control before attributing gains to additional candidates.

### 5. Hybrid Reasoning Agents

- **LLM Score**: 0
- **Keyword Score**: 1
- **Authors**: Jiaru Zou, Yunzhe Qi, Sirui Chen, Federico Bianchi, Shang Zhu, Pan Lu, Jingrui He, James Zou
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18861v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-10-13 17:59:31
- **Comment**: 45 pages, 12 figures
- **Topic Keywords**: search
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> How can we build an agent that thinks beyond human language, yet still acts through it in the external environment? Modern LLM agents still reason and interact through text, while latent reasoning offers an efficient internal medium but lacks native tool access and reasoning transparency. We bridge these two directions with Hybrid Reasoning Agents (HybridAgents), which dynamically navigate between latent reasoning and text-based interaction throughout a unified generation process. Architecturally, a lightweight HybridLink enables seamless transitions between text and latent modes, while the agent's continuous working memory preserves lossless hybrid context across multiple turns. These designs preserve tool-integrated agentic capabilities while making latent thoughts directly inspectable. Building on this architecture, we further develop dedicated post-training algorithms for hybrid reasoning: HybridSFT stabilizes tool-integrated hybrid generation by teaching the backbone to coordinate with HybridLink, then HybridRL scales reasoning by learning when to think in latent space and when to act through text. We evaluate HybridAgent at 3 model scales across 11 benchmarks covering mathematics, science, medicine, search, and agentic reasoning. Compared with frontier LLM Agents and agentic frameworks spanning both text-based and latent methods, HybridAgent consistently improves performance by 10.4%, while delivering 35.5%-54.7% token reduction, 1.8x-2.6x faster inference, and 2.7x-3.7x cost savings. Code and data are available at https://github.com/togethercomputer/HybridAgent.

- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18813v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-10-13 17:48:49
- **Comment**: 18 pages, 3 figures
- **Topic Keywords**: search
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> Harness self-evolution revises an agent's software scaffolding, including prompts, memory, workflows, and executable components, while keeping model weights fixed. We formalize a feedback-driven process in which accepted revisions persist across runs. The framework separates update targets from proposal and admission mechanisms. It distinguishes changes to interpreted state from revisions to tools and code, repository-level control logic, and plugin composition. We characterize search through evolutionary optimization and examine its reliability through continual PAC-learning. Under explicit assumptions, the analysis yields an expected generalization bound, a retention bound conditional on historical regression budgets, and a high-probability admission rule using fresh, independent validation data. We examine how persistent harness revisions affect knowledge sharing and organizational workflows, with implications for scientific reliability, physical safety, and institutional accountability. Cases from prior work illustrate model--harness co-design, distributed execution, and plugin reuse. Reliable deployment requires continual validation as task distributions and evaluators change, with independent safeguards for learning, runtime privileges, and consequential actions. We provide a comprehensive 66-page discussion of related work, theory, algorithms, evaluation, applications, and the broader impact of Harness RSI, together with continuously updated code and supplementary materials, at https://github.com/zgcagi/Harness-RSI.

### 7. SEAR: Self-Evolving Agentic Robotics

- **LLM Score**: 0
- **Keyword Score**: 1
- **Authors**: Xinyi Wang, Bangzheng Li, Hanyu Wang, Lin Zhang, Andrea Yaoyun Cui, Zhenggang Tang, Shyamal Buch, Dejia Xu
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18792v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-10-13 17:40:20
- **Comment**: 16 pages
- **Topic Keywords**: search
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> Frontier language-model agents can now improve robots on their own: they write control code, test it, and keep what works as skills and memories for the next task. We introduce SEAR (Self-Evolving Agentic Robotics), a framework and benchmark built around two questions. Is the agent really learning, or just trying more often? And does what it learns still help on new tasks, in a new simulator, and on a real robot? The framework runs an agent in three modes: it writes code that becomes the policy (code-as-policy), acts live in the control loop (agent-as-policy), or works as a researcher that fine-tunes a vision-language-action model (auto-research). The benchmark adds controls that separate retained experience from repeated attempts, familiar tasks, privileged simulator state, larger budgets, and luck. Across seven frontier language models, 330 simulated and five physical tasks, and over four years of agent runtime, we highlight three findings. First, the learning curve of a robotic agent depends both on the overall time budget and the task difficulty relative to model capability. Harder tasks make longer sessions worthwhile; on easier tasks, re-rolling short attempts does just as well. Second, control code and shared routines transfer across tasks and simulators, while task-specific solutions do not. On a real robot, we observe a performance gap between agent-designed practice and novel human-designed scenes. Third, different models evolve more effectively in different ways. Some do best acting live, others writing code first. And a text-only model keeps pace with a comparable multimodal one: perception helps with error assessment, but agentic reasoning and tool use can matter more. We release the benchmark and codebase to support future research.

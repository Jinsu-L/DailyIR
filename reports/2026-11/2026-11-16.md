#### T.A.R.G.E.T. Summary (from Abstract)
- **Topic**: Intent‑Driven Multimodal Search System for Image‑Text Queries in User‑Generated Content Communities
- **Aim**: Improve relevance judgments by explicitly modeling user retrieval intent and integrating intent‑aware learning and reranking into the search pipeline
- **Rationale**: Reranking alone cannot recover intent‑relevant results that are absent from the initial candidate set; a formal intent taxonomy and intent‑specific relevance criteria are needed to guide data construction, representation learning, and ranking
- **Ground**: Hierarchical intent taxonomy; offline agentic workflow for supervised signal generation; intent‑aware contrastive learning, graded ranking, and distribution distillation for embedding training; lightweight reranker conditioned on predicted intent
- **Experiment**: Deployed at Xiaohongshu on ~10 billion entries; P50 latency 1.07 s; A/B test results: +28.17% Top‑4 CTR, +18.96% overall CTR, +12.56% average time spent per user on search result pages
- **Takeaway**: Explicit intent modeling combined with intent‑aware learning and reranking yields substantial engagement gains while preserving real‑time, industrial‑scale performance

#### Abstract
> Multimodal search with image-text queries helps users find notes in user-generated content (UGC) communities and serves as a key search tool for multimodal search agents. For such queries, query-note relevance depends on the user's retrieval intent and cannot be determined by visual or topical similarity alone. Simply reranking visual search results cannot fully address this challenge, as notes relevant to the user's intent may be missing from the initial candidate set. This motivates explicit intent modeling to align candidate retrieval and reranking under consistent relevance criteria. We present IntentFlow, an intent-driven real-time multimodal search system deployed at Xiaohongshu over an index on the order of ten billion entries. IntentFlow represents retrieval objectives with a hierarchical intent taxonomy and uses intent-specific relevance criteria to guide data construction, representation learning, and reranking. An offline agentic data workflow acquires candidates and verifies their relevance under these criteria to produce training supervision. The embedding model combines intent-aware contrastive learning, graded ranking, and distribution distillation, while a compact reranker assesses query-candidate evidence conditioned on predicted hierarchical intents. Evaluations demonstrate that IntentFlow delivers substantial gains in search engagement while supporting real-time search at industrial scale. In a user-level A/B test on the Xiaohongshu platform, IntentFlow outperforms the existing production system based on visual search followed by reranking, achieving relative gains of 28.17% in Top-4 query click-through rate, 18.96% in query click-through rate, and 12.56% in average time spent on search results pages per user. Production monitoring records an end-to-end P50 latency of 1.07 s.

### 2. DetouR: Plugging Association Routes into Search Agents for Multi-hop Question Answering

#### T.A.R.G.E.T. Summary (from Full Text)
- **Topic**: DetouR: Plug‑in for Multi‑Hop Retrieval‑Augmented QA
- **Aim**: Enhance retrieval‑augmented QA by exploiting association links and preserving evidence chains to increase coverage and retention within a fixed query budget
- **Rationale**: Semantic search often discards linked evidence that is crucial for answering questions; association routes can recover a large portion of missed gold passages (53–73 % on HotpotQA, 39–78 % on 2Wiki). Near‑repeat queries waste budget and hurt retention; rerouting them to linked pages can recover new evidence
- **Ground**: Formal two‑stage retrieval pipeline with target discovery (γ) and query realization (φ); coverage and retention defined as probabilities of retrieving and keeping all gold passages; empirical evidence shows association routes outperform semantic routes and that merging strategies (RRF, round‑robin) affect retention
- **Experiment**: (1) HotpotQA & 2Wiki experiments with BM25 retriever, 5 follow‑ups per route, oracle selection; association routes retrieve more gold evidence; (2) Context construction study with τ retrieval lists, merging strategies (RRF, round‑robin, RRF‑no‑near‑repeats); near‑repeats (~25 %) rarely add gold; RRF peaks after 2–3 calls; (3) DetouR plug‑in: Link Rerouting replaces near‑repeats using LightGBM LambdaRank to predict best linked page; Chain‑aware Context Selection uses personalized PageRank and round‑robin ordering to keep evidence chains; evaluated on same datasets, showing improved completeness without extra cost
- **Takeaway**: DetouR turns wasteful near‑repeat queries into productive association steps and preserves logical evidence chains, yielding higher coverage and retention in retrieval‑augmented QA while keeping query budget constant

#### Abstract
> Search agents answer multi-hop questions by interleaving reasoning with retrieval, deciding at each step what to search next. Existing agents follow semantic routes: they infer what is missing and phrase it as a query, reaching only what the model can name. Yet the decisive page is often one the question never names, frequently just one hyperlink away from evidence already retrieved. Following such association routes reaches more gold pages than semantic search under the same budget. Exploiting them raises two challenges: obtaining the association structure without costly offline graph construction, and keeping linked evidence in the reader's limited context, where relevance- and fusion-based selection discards it. We propose DetouR, a plug-in that reuses the links arriving with retrieved pages and adds no search or LLM call. Link Rerouting replaces near-repeat queries with routes to linked pages, chosen by a lightweight learned scorer; Chain-aware Context Selection runs personalized PageRank over these links so that evidence chains reach the reader intact. Across four agents, DetouR improves EM by 4.0 points on 2Wiki and 1.4 on HotpotQA on average. We also find that heavier LLM models struggle with this non-semantic evidence, adding little over these simple selectors or even falling behind. In summary, our work reveals association routes as a cheap complement to semantic routes that current search agents overlook: added as a plug-in, they improve every agent we test, in replay and in live runs, at no extra search or LLM cost.

- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19220v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-11-14 07:26:16
- **Comment**: 17 pages, 2 figures. The format of ACL rolling review
- **Topic Keywords**: query, pairwise, relevance, rag, retrieval, search
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> Selecting context subsets is increasingly important in modern LLM inference, yet what makes a subset effective remains unclear. We study this question in the controlled setting of few-shot demonstration selection. We examine a hypothesis that demonstrations should collectively provide task-relevant information needed by a target query. Assuming that the query representation provides a useful proxy for these requirements, we formulate selection as sparse reconstruction and instantiate it with Residual Explanation Search (RES), a Matching-Pursuit-style diagnostic procedure. Across seven benchmarks and eight open-weight LLMs, no retrieval objective is universally best. RES outperforms MMR in 34 of 56 model--dataset pairs, with its clearest advantage on sentiment tasks, suggesting that query-conditioned collective reconstruction may capture useful complementarity that pairwise dissimilarity misses. Relevance-based retrieval remains strong on template-heavy reasoning benchmarks, while all retrieval methods underperform zero-shot prediction on average on StrategyQA, where a solution-reconstruction analysis is consistent with the query not exposing the required external knowledge. Target-model hidden states further show that retrieval objectives interact differently with representation spaces: middle-layer representations generally favor similarity retrieval, whereas last-layer representations are often stronger for residual- and diversity-based selection. These findings support a conditional, representation-dependent account of context subset quality rather than a universally superior selector.

### 4. UniGraph: A Unified Graph-based Index for Dynamic Hybrid Dense-Sparse Vector Retrieval

- **LLM Score**: 0
- **Keyword Score**: 13
- **Authors**: Keyu Chen, Xiaoyao Zhong, Jiabao Jin, Peng Cheng, Wangze Ni, Jingkuan Song, Heng Tao Shen
- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20758v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-11-16 09:06:58
- **Comment**: Under ICDE 2027 Review, 14 pages
- **Topic Keywords**: query, ranking, rerank, retrieval, rank, search
- **Reason**: LLM scoring failed.

#### Abstract
> Hybrid dense-sparse vector retrieval combines semantic signals from dense representations with lexical signals from sparse representations, typically ranking items by a query-dependent weighted sum of dense and sparse vector distances. Existing systems commonly search separate dense and sparse vector indexes and rerank the union of their candidates, incurring two retrieval passes and potentially discarding items that rank highly only after the semantic and lexical signals are combined. Constructing a graph-based index directly under a hybrid distance can mitigate this premature candidate loss. However, because varying the hybrid weight alters the underlying neighborhood relationships, a single static graph built at a fixed weight cannot accommodate dynamic query-specific weights. Therefore, we propose a Unified Hybrid Graph (UniGraph), which merges neighbor sets computed at multiple hybrid weights into a single bounded-degree graph index, enabling hybrid vector retrieval with different query-specific weights without index reconstruction. We further develop an adaptive weight-guided search framework that chooses between sparse- and dense-guided entry points according to the query weight, together with a dense-first hybrid distance pruning strategy that avoids unnecessary exact sparse distance computations. Extensive experiments show that UniGraph provides a favorable Recall-QPS trade-off across different query weights. In the mixed-weight setting, UniGraph delivers 27.6%~52.3% higher QPS than the fastest baseline on each dataset at Recall@100 greater than or equal to 0.95. UniGraph has been deployed at scale in Ant Group's production environment, where hybrid retrieval with query-dependent weights is in strong demand.

- **URL**: <http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20979v1>
- **Submitted**: 2026-11-16 12:14:15

#### Abstract
> LLM-based natural-language interfaces to relational databases are gated by a schema-retrieval step: the generator can only be as good as the slice of schema it is shown. On a database with hundreds of tables, that slice must be selected and the selection is two-sided---omit a needed table and the query becomes unrecoverable, include too many and the irrelevant ones act as noise that degrades the model's output. When an agentic retriever that lets the model inspect the schema can be too costly or too slow, the fallback is an embedder that ranks tables by cosine similarity and keeps a top-k. This fallback has two weaknesses: a fixed budget is suboptimal question by question, and embedding similarity overlooks foreign-key-linked tables that are necessary for joins. We present Adaptive-Elbow Graph Traversal (AEGT, ElbowHop for short), a property-graph table retriever that addresses both: an adaptive elbow cutoff replaces the fixed budget with a per-question one under a single aggressiveness parameter, and bidirectional foreign-key traversal deterministically recovers the bridge tables embedding similarity misses. We evaluate the retriever on 3 corpora of increasing candidate-pool size (BIRD Mini-Dev queried per database, 3--13 tables; its eleven schemas merged, 74; and a merged Spider-train graph, 546) and isolate the two mechanisms under a per-question budget-matched protocol. Across these settings the adaptive elbow emerges as the dominant mechanism---at matched recall it roughly doubles precision over a fixed budget on the larger pools, the margin widening as the pool grows---while the foreign-key traversal pays off on the join-critical bridge tables dense retrieval cannot reach, in the single-hop tight-budget case. We characterize the method as a low-cost, recall-robust first stage for schema retrieval on which a downstream selection or generation step can build.

#### Abstract
> Generative retrieval replaces nearest-neighbor search with the generation of a document's semantic ID (SID), so the SID tokenizer bounds what the retriever can recall. Common SID constructions (residual k-means, RQ-VAE, finite scalar quantization, capacity balancing) optimize criteria defined on document embeddings alone and do not control whether a query can still find its relevant documents after quantization. We propose two retrieval-oriented SID tokenizers. CARE-SID (Contrastive-Anchored Residual Encoding for Semantic IDs) quantizes in a retrieval-aligned space: a zero-initialized residual alignment network is trained with a query-document contrastive loss before quantization, an anchor loss and a reconstruction target fixed to the original embedding prevent drift and collapse, and reference-vector rating residual quantization assigns the codes. Both keep a document-only encoding interface. We judge SIDs without training a generator, by row-budget recall in a bucketing view (the partition) and a reconstruction view (the SID with its reconstruction). In offline evaluation on industrial video search data, both tokenizers achieve higher reconstruction-view recall than residual k-means, and CARE-LR improves further on CARE-SID, mainly through the listwise distillation. A comparison of two document embeddings shows that contrastive alignment helps most when the embedding has not been trained on search data, and that the embedding affects SID recall far more than the quantizer. CARE-SID is deployed in the generative retrieval stage of an industrial video search system.

#### Abstract
> In-context learning is usually analyzed as if the examples in the prompt were sampled before the learner is chosen. In deployed systems they are not: examples are retrieved, filtered, re-ranked, edited, and repeatedly tested after seeing the query and the data store. We formalize this gap by treating prompt construction as adaptive data analysis through a conditional stochastic-kernel calculus. Our main quantity is the context capacity, the mutual information or approximate max-information between the data store and the final prompt transcript. The results separate context quality from context certification: longer or more relevant contexts improve risk only when the procedure used to choose them is stable enough for selected-context evidence to transfer.

#### Abstract
> Legal reasoning in civil-law systems is not rule-lookup: the German state examination asks for a structured opinion (Gutachten) that names the governing norms, applies them to the facts, and argues to a conclusion. We introduce GPBam (German Public Law Bar-style Exam), 81 public-law examination cases with reference solutions, paired with a recitation task over 200 statutory provisions. Drawn from state examination preparation, the cases are broad and rarely repeat, and are not publicly available - we find no evidence of exact-solution memorisation in the models we evaluate, and keeping them private limits contamination. Because each reference solution's cited norms form a retrieval gold set over the very instances the essays are graded on, GPBam scores retrieval and long-form generation jointly, which makes it a benchmark for legal retrieval as well as for legal writing and lets us ask how much retrieval quality a legal opinion actually needs. Across 32 open-weight and proprietary models evaluated without retrieval, scores span 4.81-76.42 out of 100. Supplying the law helps modestly: across seven models and eight retrieval configurations, retrieval performance varies nearly threefold and buys at most 4.1 essay points, and nothing significant for three of the seven, while an oracle with 91% gold-norm recall improves scores by 2-6 points. Essay quality strongly tracks prior knowledge: verbatim statute recall, measured in a separate task, correlates at $ρ= 0.92$ over 32 models.

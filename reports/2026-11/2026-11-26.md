#### T.A.R.G.E.T. Summary (from Full Text)
- **Topic**: Heterogeneous Retrieval‑Augmented Generation (RAG) Cascades
- **Aim**: Demonstrate that lightweight, cache‑based coarse scorers can be composed into multi‑stage RAG pipelines without joint training, while improving answer quality and inference speed.
- **Rationale**: Large‑scale retrieval requires fast filtering; coarse scorers provide inexpensive pre‑selection that can be inserted before any downstream reranker, simplifying deployment and avoiding costly joint training.
- **Ground**: Architecture: frozen MiniLM‑L6‑v2 encoder; 1537‑dim pairwise feature vector; DeepSets scorer (3.39 M params) trained with pairwise margin loss. Two variants: Cache‑Pointwise (singleton score) and Cache‑Shapley (Shapley value via 10‑perm sampling). Deployment: linear‑time singleton inference, fixed‑budget selection (count, NoLiMa). Cascade definition: (g,α,h,B,R) with coarse ranker g, admitted fraction α, precise reranker h, budget B, reader R. Metrics: Rank, Retention, QA, online latency, peak memory.
- **Experiment**: Transfer experiments on MS MARCO, HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, MuSiQue show learned scorers outperform frozen cosine in NPRS and ranking; frozen cosine best on MuSiQue. Coarse‑only reader retains all gold evidence; removing surrounding text boosts QA from 22.18 % to 95.06 %. Pipeline configs: ColBERTv2 Hard, ColBERTv250→CrossEncoder, ColBERTv250→Provence, PW Hard/RRF, Sh. RRF. Key results: retention 0.47–0.91 (ColBERTv2 alone), 0.71–0.81 with second stage; QA 0.28–0.91 (ColBERTv2 alone), ≈0.67–0.74 with two‑stage; speed‑up 1.18–1.71× for single‑stage; PW cascades +12.53 pp retention, +4.60 pp QA; Sh. cascades +6.32 pp retention, +4.14 pp QA; depth‑extension +16.21 pp (ColBERTv250→CE), +21.03 pp (ColBERTv250→Prov.). Monte‑Carlo budget: training M≤5 stable; deployment singleton (M=1) best for retention (~76 %) and latency (~17 ms). Stage‑removal analysis shows impact depends on remaining reranker strength. Evidence repair/breakage probabilities quantified when moving from coarse to precise reranker.
- **Takeaway**: Cache‑based coarse scorers enable efficient, modular RAG cascades without joint training, delivering significant gains in evidence retention, QA accuracy, and inference speed across diverse datasets; singleton scoring is optimal for deployment, and cascading with strong downstream rerankers consistently improves performance.

#### Abstract
> Standalone reranker quality does not determine its value in a multistage RAG pipeline. We systematically study heterogeneous rerankers composed without joint training, spanning lexical, single-vector, late-interaction, and joint query-context scoring. Two lightweight semantic coarse scorers, Cache-Pointwise and Cache-Shapley, learn from passage relevance labels and share cacheable representations and linear-time singleton inference. Under matched candidates and budgets, we compare cascades with stage-removal references to measure evidence retention, repair and breakage, reader accuracy, and online pruning cost. On NoLiMa 32K with Gemma, inserting these scorers before CrossEncoder or Provence improves answer accuracy by 4.02-9.43 percentage points while accelerating online pruning by 1.57-1.88x relative to the corresponding reranker alone. Cache-Pointwise composed with ColBERTv2 through reciprocal rank fusion improves accuracy by 4.60 points at 1.36x speedup over direct ColBERTv2. The stage-removal comparisons reveal an asymmetry: a cascade can outperform its final reranker alone while underperforming its coarse selector alone. Two readers, a second multi-hop dataset, and three-stage extensions show that the gains depend on the reader, reranker pairing, and stage configuration. These findings demonstrate a practical route to better and faster reranking without joint training and motivate studying stage order, candidate budgets, and rank fusion as determinants of composition quality.

#### Abstract
> Data management systems organize large text collections for tasks such as finding relevant studies and extracting contract clauses. Systems make long documents searchable by splitting them into independently indexed chunks. Chunking can separate text from its identifying details. The indexing challenge is to keep each chunk independently searchable while preserving the context needed to find and rank it. Existing methods either encode that context in chunk vectors or attach it as a text prefix. Context encoded only in vectors is unavailable to text rerankers. Prompted prefixes serve both vector search and reranking, but are costly to generate at indexing time and can underperform contextual encoders. We propose ConTra (Context That Travels), a reinforcement learning framework for training lightweight prefix generators. On ConTEB and RealContextBench, ConTra-2B outperforms the prompted 9B baseline using Anthropic's method and the PPLX-4B contextual encoder, with a margin of up to 3.15 points over their best overall scores, while indexing short documents 7.74 times and 2.15 times as fast as those baselines, respectively. Post-trained only on ConTEB, it leads the standalone methods on RealContextBench after reranking and is less sensitive than the prompted 2B baseline to random and topically similar distractors. Code, datasets, and trained models are available at https://github.com/SStan1/ConTra.

#### Abstract
> Data allocation methods often use a small proxy model to select training data for a larger target model. However, this proxy can rank data differently from the target, leading to suboptimal training performance. To address this problem, we model the mismatch between the proxy and target models and incorporate it into the allocation decision. To do so, the problem is cast as a multi-fidelity bandit, where each arm is a data intervention. The proxy reward is observed every round, whereas the target reward is revealed only on rare audits of the same update. Under abrupt changes, using the audit budget early can also incur linear regret. A sliding-window policy that spreads audits over training achieves regret of order $\sqrt{Γ_T T\log T}$ where $Γ_T$ denotes the number of change points. Experimentally, we test the approach in a closed-loop proof of concept on CivilComments-WILDS, where BERT-Small guides the training of BERT-Large. Mismatch correction improves worst-group accuracy over a target-only policy with the same target-model budget. Spreading audits over training improves worst-group accuracy by 6.3 to 10.5 percentage points at the same audit count.

#### Abstract
> Cultural alignment requires large language models (LLMs) to infer often-implicit social norms from a scenario and its cultural context. Existing approaches typically adapt individual models or supply external cultural knowledge, while learning norm inference independently of downstream response generation remains underexplored. We introduce NORMCRAFT, a modular framework that decouples norm inference from response generation. Through norm-centric multi-view supervision, we train an LLM as a norm generator to recover shared norms from prospective, adherence, and violation scenarios without downstream response labels. The generated norms guide a separate, frozen LLM (the responder). With Qwen3.5-4B as the responder, NORMCRAFT outperforms prompting and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) baselines on four of five downstream metrics. On SafeWorld, its responses reflect the reference norms more often (Coverage) and more precisely (Faithfulness), exceeding Responder SFT by 18.0 and 14.1 percentage points, respectively. Direct norm evaluation also shows higher norm quality than the base and few-shot generators. The same trained generator improves cultural alignment on SafeWorld across fourteen frozen LLMs spanning five families and 0.8B to 27B parameters, with the largest gains for the smallest models.
